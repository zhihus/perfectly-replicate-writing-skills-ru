# Writing Formulas — 14 Writing Modes

Reference for 14 writing formulas/formats. Each formula has a specific structure, optimal use case, and compatibility markers.

## 1. PAS — Problem-Agitate-Solution

**Structure:**
1. **Problem** — Name a specific pain point the reader has
2. **Agitate** — Amplify the discomfort, make it felt
3. **Solution** — Present your answer as relief

**Best for:** Sales copy, marketing emails, landing pages
**Voice fit:** Empathetic + authoritative
**Tone match:** Conversational with emotional resonance

**Example shape:**
> You sit down to write. The cursor blinks. Nothing comes. [Problem]
> You feel the deadline pressing. Doubt creeps in. Each blank page is a little failure. [Agitate]
> There's a better way — and it takes 5 minutes to learn. [Solution]

---

## 2. SCAR — Situation-Complication-Action-Resolution

**Structure:**
1. **Situation** — Establish the status quo
2. **Complication** — Introduce a problem or tension
3. **Action** — What was done to address it
4. **Resolution** — The outcome

**Best for:** Case studies, narrative essays, project recaps
**Voice fit:** Narrative, confident, slightly dramatic
**Tone match:** Storytelling register

---

## 3. QUEST — Question-Understand-Evaluate-Solution-Transformation

**Structure:**
1. **Question** — Frame the core inquiry
2. **Understand** — Explore the context and background
3. **Evaluate** — Weigh options, pros and cons
4. **Solution** — Present the chosen path
5. **Transformation** — The before/after picture

**Best for:** Educational content, consulting frameworks, analysis pieces
**Voice fit:** Analytical, teacherly, thorough

---

## 4. AIDA — Attention-Interest-Desire-Action

**Structure:**
1. **Attention** — Grab with a hook (stat, question, bold claim)
2. **Interest** — Build curiosity, provide context
3. **Desire** — Make the reader want the outcome
4. **Action** — Clear call to action

**Best for:** Ad copy, sales pages, product launches
**Voice fit:** Persuasive, confident
**Tone match:** Direct, urgent when appropriate

---

## 5. FAB — Features-Advantages-Benefits

**Structure:**
1. **Feature** — What it is (neutral fact)
2. **Advantage** — What it does (functional improvement)
3. **Benefit** — What it means for the reader (emotional outcome)

**Best for:** Product descriptions, comparison guides, technical specs
**Voice fit:** Clear, direct, customer-oriented

**Example shape:**
> This laptop has an M4 chip [Feature], so it renders 4K video 3x faster [Advantage], meaning you finish edits before lunch and actually enjoy your evening [Benefit].

---

## 6. Before-After-Bridge (BAB)

**Structure:**
1. **Before** — The painful or inadequate state
2. **After** — The improved, desirable state
3. **Bridge** — How to get from one to the other

**Best for:** Transformation stories, coaching copy, before/after comparisons
**Voice fit:** Empathetic, aspirational

---

## 7. SSS — Star-Story-Solution

**Structure:**
1. **Star** — Lead with the compelling result
2. **Story** — How we got there
3. **Solution** — The system/approach that made it possible

**Best for:** Case studies, testimonials, proof pieces
**Voice fit:** Credible, specific, evidence-driven

---

## 8. Problem-Agitate-Solution-Proof (PASP)

Same as PAS + a proof/evidence paragraph after the solution.

**Best for:** High-commitment offers, skeptical audiences
**Voice fit:** PAS + data-driven credibility

---

## 9. Inverted Pyramid

**Structure:**
1. **Lead** — Most important info (who, what, when, where, why)
2. **Body** — Supporting details, context
3. **Tail** — Background, nuance, less critical info

**Best for:** News articles, announcements, press releases
**Voice fit:** Neutral, informative, scannable

---

## 10. Listicle / Structured List

**Structure:**
- Numbered or bulleted items with a unifying theme
- Each item: subhead + 2-5 sentences of content
- Strong opener and closer

**Best for:** Blog posts, guides, shareable content
**Voice fit:** Approachable, opinionated, easy to digest

---

## 11. Narrative Arc (Freytag's Pyramid)

**Structure:**
1. **Exposition** — Setup, context, characters
2. **Rising Action** — Building tension
3. **Climax** — Turning point
4. **Falling Action** — Consequences
5. **Denouement** — Resolution, lesson

**Best for:** Personal essays, long-form narrative, memoir
**Voice fit:** Literary, reflective, emotionally resonant

---

## 12. LATCH — Location-Alphabet-Time-Category-Hierarchy

**Structure:** Organize information by one of five organizing principles:
1. **Location** — Geographical or spatial
2. **Alphabet** — A to Z
3. **Time** — Chronological
4. **Category** — By type or theme
5. **Hierarchy** — By importance or size

**Best for:** Reference guides, directories, encyclopedic content
**Voice fit:** Neutral, structured, utility-focused

---

## 13. Problem-Cause-Solution (PCS)

**Structure:**
1. **Problem** — What's wrong
2. **Cause** — Why it happens (root cause analysis)
3. **Solution** — How to fix the root cause

**Best for:** Technical explainers, troubleshooting guides, system design docs
**Voice fit:** Logical, technical, patient

---

## 14. The 4Ps — Picture-Problem-Promise-Prove

**Structure:**
1. **Picture** — Paint the ideal scenario
2. **Problem** — Name what prevents it
3. **Promise** — What's possible with the solution
4. **Prove** — Evidence it works

**Best for:** Persuasive long-form sales, vision pieces
**Voice fit:** Visionary + grounded (balance of aspiration and proof)

---

## Formula Selection Guide

When recommending formulas during Phase 2 analysis:

| Author Profile | Favored Formulas | Avoid |
|----------------|------------------|-------|
| Conversational, short sentences | PAS, BAB, Listicle | Inverted Pyramid, LATCH |
| Analytical, data-heavy | PCS, QUEST, AIDA | Narrative Arc, SSS |
| Narrative, literary | Narrative Arc, SCAR, QUEST | LATCH, Inverted Pyramid |
| Instructional, teacherly | FAB, PCS, How-To variants | PAS (too salesy) |
| Persuasive, opinionated | AIDA, PAS, 4Ps, SSS | LATCH, Inverted Pyramid |
| Poetic, reflective | Narrative Arc, BAB | AIDA, FAB |

Rate each formula for the author on the 4-point scale:
- **天生适合** — Born fit. The formula amplifies the author's natural strengths.
- **可以驾驭** — Can handle. The author's style can adapt to this formula with some adjustment.
- **不太适合** — Poor fit. Significant style tension; not recommended.
- **冲突** — Actively clashes. The formula would require the author to write against their nature.
